## ⚖️ 1. Homicide Definitions (FBI 2005 Consensus)

In 2005, the FBI Behavioral Analysis Unit hosted a multi-disciplinary Serial Murder Symposium to standardize operational definitions:

```
                                  Homicide Classifications
                                             │
      ┌──────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┐
      ▼                                      ▼                                      ▼
Serial Murder                           Mass Murder                            Spree Murder
├── 2 or more separate events           ├── 4 or more victims                  ├── 2 or more locations
├── Separate locations                  ├── Single event / location            ├── Continuous, single event
└── Cooling-off period between acts      └── No cooling-off period              └── No emotional cooling-off period
```

* **Serial Murder**: The unlawful killing of two or more victims by the same offender(s) in separate events, with an emotional **"cooling-off period"** between homicides (hours, weeks, months, or years).
* **Mass Murder**: The killing of four or more victims at one location within a single event (e.g., workplace shootings, school attacks).
* **Spree Murder**: Homicides at two or more locations with almost no emotional cooling-off period, representing a continuous violent rampage.

## 🎯 2. Holmes & Holmes Serial Offender Typology

Criminologists Ronald and Stephen Holmes categorized serial murder motivations into four primary typologies:

```
                            Holmes & Holmes Typology
                                       │
     ┌──────────────────┬──────────────┴───────────────┬──────────────────┐
     ▼                  ▼                              ▼                  ▼
  Visionary         Mission-Oriented              Hedonistic            Power/Control
(Psychotic)        (Ideological)                 (Gratification)       (Dominance)
                                            ┌──────────┼──────────┐
                                            ▼          ▼          ▼
                                          Lust       Thrill    Comfort/Gain
```

| Typology | Primary Motivation | Mental State | Crime Scene Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Visionary** | Responding to auditory/visual hallucinations or delusions (demonic/divine voices). | Psychotic / Severely disordered. | Chaotic, disorganized, sudden, no effort to conceal body or evidence. |
| **Mission-Oriented** | Seeking to eradicate a specific group of people deemed "undesirable" (e.g., prostitutes, homeless, ethnic groups). | Non-psychotic; rationale based on distorted morality. | Organized, controlled, specific geographic targeting, targeted selection. |
| **Hedonistic: Lust** | Direct sexual gratification derived from the killing process, torture, or post-mortem acts. | Non-psychotic; fantasy & sadistic driven. | Close contact physical violence, mutilation, sexual assault, organized/disorganized mix. |
| **Hedonistic: Thrill** | Seeking internal excitement, adrenaline rush, or "high" from inflicting terror. | Non-psychotic; thrill-seeking. | Prolonged process, torture, victim fear induction; ceases once victim dies. |
| **Hedonistic: Comfort/Gain** | Material, financial, or personal comfort (e.g., insurance fraud, inheritance, poisoners). | Rational, calculating. | Clean, methodical, poison/firearms, low violence signature. |
| **Power / Control** | Absolute dominance, mastery, and control over another human being's life and death. | Non-psychotic; Narcissistic/Psychopathic. | Highly organized, prolonged captivity, bondage, extended torture signature. |

## 🔞 3. Sexual Homicide & Sadism Typologies (Hazelwood Model)

FBI Special Agent Roy Hazelwood categorized sexual homicide offenders based on the underlying psychological dynamic:

1. **Power-Reassurance (Compensatory)**:
   * Offender suffers from deep feelings of sexual inadequacy. Fantasy involves victim falling in love with them. Uses minimal force; apologetic; flees if victim forcefully resists.
2. **Power-Assertive (Entitled)**:
   * Offender views violence as an expression of masculinity and dominance. High confidence, aggressive, repeated assaults; escalates violence to maintain control.
3. **Anger-Retaliatory (Displaced Rage)**:
   * Offender harbors intense rage toward a specific gender or group (often stemming from rejection). Attack is brutal, impulsive, and aimed at punishing the victim.
4. **Anger-Excitation (Sadistic)**:
   * The most dangerous offender profile. Sexual excitement is directly derived from inflicting physical and psychological pain, fear, and suffering. Highly planned, organized, and secret torturer.

## 🔥 4. Arson & Mass Violence Typologies

### Arson Typology (FBI Crime Classification Manual)
1. **Vandalism**: Malicious mischief, usually committed by juveniles/adolescents in groups.
2. **Excitement / Thrill**: Setting fires for thrill, attention, or playing the "hero" firefighter.
3. **Revenge**: Retaliation for perceived personal, institutional, or romantic slights.
4. **Crime Concealment**: Setting fire to destroy fingerprints, DNA, or cover a homicide/burglary.
5. **Profit / Fraud**: Insurance collection or commercial arson.

### Mass Casualty & Active Shooter Threat Analysis
Modern BAU research (such as the FBI's *Active Shooter Study*) highlights that mass violence attackers rarely "snap" spontaneously. They follow a predictable behavioral trajectory:
* **Pre-Attack Indicators**: Purchasing arms beyond financial means, writing manifestos, social isolation, obsession with past mass shooters.
* **Leakage**: Broadcasting intent online or to peers prior to attack.
